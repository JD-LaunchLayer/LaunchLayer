Slow Laptop in Wickford? An SSD Upgrade Is Usually the Fix

Useful Tips 16 Sep Jordan Duggins

If your laptop takes two minutes to reach the desktop, freezes when you open Photos, or sounds like a tiny tractor every time you save a file, it is probably not “too old”. It is probably still running a spinning hard drive.

At LaunchLayer in Wickford I see this every week: a perfectly decent i5 or Ryzen machine from 2018–2021, 8 or 16GB of RAM, and a 5400rpm disk that cannot keep up with Windows 11. A solid-state drive (SSD) upgrade is the single biggest speed change I can make without throwing the laptop away.

This is not the same job as a full clean because it will not turn on, and it is not automatically a reason to buy new. It is a targeted parts swap with your files cloned across.

How to tell it is the hard drive, not Windows “being Windows”

Three clues show up together:

  1. The drive light (or the chassis) chatters when you copy a folder or open Outlook. SSDs are silent. Mechanical disks seek.
  2. Task Manager → Performance → Disk sits at 100% while the CPU is bored. That is the disk queue, not a virus by default.
  3. It was fast in 2019 and miserable in 2026 with the same apps. Windows, Chrome and Teams grew. The platter did not.

Overheating can also make a machine feel slow. If the fans scream and it only crawls after twenty minutes, read the overheating guide first. An SSD will not fix thermal throttling.

A machine that takes five minutes to boot and clicks or grind as it does so is a dying disk, not a slow one. Stop using it and treat it as a data recovery problem. Cloning a failing platter “to see if it helps” is how family photos disappear on a Tuesday.

What an SSD upgrade actually involves

I do not “install Windows from scratch” unless you ask. The usual Wickford job is:

  1. Image your existing drive (files, programmes, licence, desktop clutter and all).
  2. Fit a 2.5” SATA SSD or an M.2 NVMe module, depending on the chassis.
  3. Clone the image, expand the partition, and confirm it boots.
  4. Run a health check so we are not cloning a dying disk onto a nice new one blindly.

Turnaround is typically same day or next day if the part is in the UK stock I use. You keep your PIN, your Outlook profile, and the folder of PDFs you could not recreate.

SATA vs NVMe — you cannot will a slot into existence

Thin 2016–2019 office laptops often only take a 2.5” SATA SSD. That is still five to ten times faster than a spinning disk for boot and documents. Newer machines and most custom PCs take M.2 NVMe, which is the right choice for photo work and games.

I will not sell you a Gen5 stick for a Gen3 slot. The motherboard decides the ceiling. If you are building a new box rather than upgrading a lid, the custom PC page is the cleaner route.

Capacity is the other honest conversation. 256GB felt huge in 2016. In 2026 it fills with Teams caches and phone backups. 500GB is the floor I recommend for a home laptop; 1TB if you keep raw photos or a Steam library on the machine.

Will I lose my files?

Not if the clone is done properly. Your Windows login, Outlook profiles and Steam library stay put. The old disk can be kept as a USB backup for a fortnight if you want a safety net. A clone is not a backup habit, though — it is a one-off copy of whatever was on the machine that day. If the only photos live on that laptop, say so before we start.

MacBooks are a different conversation. Soldered storage on Apple silicon cannot be swapped. Intel Macs with a removable SSD still can; that sits on the MacBook speed guide. If you are not sure which Mac you have, MacBook repair in Wickford is the right first stop.
