Laptop Won’t Turn On or Runs Slow? Fixes Before You Replace It

Useful Tips

Written By Jordan Duggins

If the lid is a black rectangle — no lights, no fan, nothing — or Chrome takes so long you can put the kettle on, you do not need a new laptop yet. Most “dead” or crawling machines that land on the Wickford bench are one or two real faults, not a write-off.

Phase 1: The total blackout (it won’t turn on)

No lights, no sound, no signs of life? Start here before you assume the board is gone.

  • The charger test. Is the wall socket on? Any kinks or fraying on the lead? Try another socket. If the brick has a green or blue light, check it is actually lit.
  • The power drain. Unplug the charger. If the battery comes out, remove it. Hold the power button for 30 full seconds. Reconnect the charger and press power. Static on the board stops a surprising number of boots.
  • The hidden screen fault. Sometimes the laptop is on and the panel is dead. Listen for a fan or look for a tiny power LED. Plug a TV or monitor in via HDMI. If you see the desktop, the computer is fine — you need laptop screen repair in Wickford.
  • Listen to the beeps. A sequence of beeps or flashing LEDs is a hardware error code. Three rapid beeps often point at memory (RAM), not a “dead” machine.

Phase 2: Painfully slow (performance fixes)

If it turns on but feels stuck in 2005, these are the three bottlenecks I see across Essex.

1. The hard-drive bottleneck (the usual culprit). A laptop more than three or four years old often still has a spinning hard disk. Those platters wear, chatter, and queue every click.

  • The fix: a solid-state drive (SSD) upgrade. It is the single most useful hardware change for a slow Windows laptop — boots in seconds rather than minutes, with your files cloned across.

2. Memory (RAM) starvation. Teams plus twenty Chrome tabs will flatten 4GB or even 8GB. The machine then “swaps” to a slow disk and freezes.

  • The fix: 16GB of RAM, where the chassis allows it, so it can actually multitask.

3. Startup junk. Apps that launch with Windows eat the processor before you have opened an email.

  • The fix: Ctrl + Shift + Esc → Startup apps, and switch off anything you do not need at boot (Spotify, Skype, manufacturer extras).

Phase 3: The heat wave

If it sounds like a jet and the underside is hot, cooling has given up. The processor slows itself on purpose (thermal throttling) so it does not cook. Blocked vents or dried thermal paste are a workshop clean, not a new laptop.

Overheating and a slow disk often travel together. If the fans scream and the drive chatters, say both when you book so we do not treat one and leave the other.

Repair vs replace: when to stop

I would rather repair than replace, but you have to be realistic. If the machine is over seven years old and needs a new screen, a battery, and an SSD, the quote can overtake the value of the laptop. That is an honest conversation, not a sales pitch for a new box.

Frequently asked questions

The fans spin but it won’t turn on — is it dead?
Not always. Fans and a power light mean the board is getting some power. Try an HDMI monitor. A desktop on the TV usually means a screen or cable, which is a screen repair, not a new laptop.

Battery or charger?
Known-good socket first, then the brick light. A dark brick is a PSU. A machine that only lives on mains often needs a battery — we will say which after the free diagnostic.

Will an SSD fix a slow laptop?
If it still has a spinning disk, usually yes. It will not fix heat or a dying board. See the SSD upgrade guide or book the bench.
