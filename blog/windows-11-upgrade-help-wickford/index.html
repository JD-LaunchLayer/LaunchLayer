Still Blocked from Windows 11 in Wickford? TPM, RAM and Your Options

Useful Tips 28 Oct The LaunchLayer workshop

Good news first: a message saying your PC “can’t run Windows 11” doesn’t mean it’s broken. The compatibility checker has spotted something it wants changed. Sometimes that’s a setting we can switch on, and sometimes a small upgrade helps. Either way, there’s a sensible way forward.

If you’re in Wickford and your PC says “this processor isn’t supported” or “TPM 2.0 must be enabled” (TPM is a small security chip inside your PC), you’re not alone. Windows 10 reached end of support on 14 October 2025, when Microsoft stopped its regular updates. The wider background is in our Windows 10 end-of-support piece; this page covers the upgrade.

There’s still time. Home PCs enrolled in Microsoft’s consumer Extended Security Updates keep getting security updates until 12 October 2027. (ESU is Microsoft’s way of keeping Windows 10 PCs protected.) Microsoft’s ESU page says you can enrol any time until the programme ends on that date, and coverage continues through it if you’re already enrolled. Consumer ESU isn’t for a work PC that’s domain-joined or managed by company IT, meaning one your employer’s IT team runs. Not enrolled yet? It’s worth doing soon, as Microsoft says PCs without these updates are more at risk. Worried about viruses? Our virus removal is a separate job from this upgrade.

Your three options:

  • Switch on what’s needed, then upgrade. We’ll turn on any setting that’s off.
  • Give it a boost first. Faster storage and more memory.
  • Keep your files, move to a newer PC.

Our Windows 11 upgrade and compatibility fix is from £69, after a free diagnostic. Not sure which option fits? Call us on 07367 652987 and we’ll talk it through.

What the checker is flagging

  • TPM 2.0. The security chip we mentioned. On many PCs from 2018–2020 it’s there but switched off in the firmware settings (the PC’s built-in start-up menu), where it may be called “PTT” or “fTPM”. If it’s there, we can switch it on.
  • Secure Boot. A start-up safety setting that checks the software starting up is trusted. It’s sometimes left off after a reinstall, and we can usually switch it back on.
  • RAM. Your PC’s short-term memory. You need at least 4GB, so the checker flags anything lower. 8GB is the practical minimum in 2026, and 16GB helps if you keep lots of tabs open.
  • The processor list. Your processor (the PC’s brain) usually can’t be upgraded without a new main board. A good i5-6500 or early Ryzen machine may never be on Microsoft’s list. Workarounds exist, and for a home PC we’ll explain the pros and cons first. If you use it for work email, we’ll talk through what’s safest.

Is it slow too? A Wickford SSD upgrade swaps an old hard drive for an SSD, a much faster type of storage. Add 16GB of RAM and Windows 11 becomes comfortable to use.

What we’ll actually do

First, we’ll check what your PC has and what’s switched off. Before any upgrade, we check your drive and take a full copy of your files, so your photos and documents stay safe.

“Copilot+ PCs” are Microsoft’s newest AI-feature machines, a nice extra rather than a must-have. Our 2026 AI PC note explains more.

Options, in order

  1. Enable TPM or Secure Boot and upgrade in place (install over your current setup). Best when the hardware is fine.
  2. SSD and RAM, then upgrade. Best when the hardware is fine but tired.
  3. Keep the files, replace the box. Best when the processor isn’t on the list and you want something dependable. See our buying advice, or a custom build.

If a desktop crashes partway through, we’ll check its temperatures and drive first. A dusty one gets a thermal clean before we start.

A laptop that won’t last through an hour-long upgrade may have a charging fault, or an underpowered charger. We’ll use a compatible charger.

Licences, files, and buying Windows again

Most home PCs already have a digital licence (proof your copy of Windows is genuine) tied to the motherboard, the main circuit board. An upgrade in place keeps it, and a clean install (a fresh Windows) on the same board usually activates online. A brand-new custom PC needs its own licence, and we’ll explain the options and costs first.

When your drive is healthy, your files survive an upgrade in place. If it isn’t, we’ll look at it before copying anything and explain the data recovery options and costs. Your files are kept safe first.
