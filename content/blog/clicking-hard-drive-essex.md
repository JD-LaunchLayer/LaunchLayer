A clicking, grinding or repeated beep-beep from a desktop or an older laptop is the disk asking to die. It is not “Windows being slow”. It is not a job for Disk Cleanup. Every extra minute of use can turn a recoverable drive into a platter we cannot read.

## What you are hearing

Mechanical 3.5" and 2.5" drives park and unpark heads. When the heads cannot find a track they retry — that is the click. A healthy SSD does not do this. If a “silent” laptop starts ticking, it still has a platter in it, or a dying fan; fans whoosh and scrape, they do not metronome.

Windows may also show:

- Disk at 100% in Task Manager while nothing is copying
- “Windows repaired your drive” after every boot
- SMART warnings, “care”, “predictive failure”

A [blue screen](/blog/windows-blue-screen-fix-essex) with disk in-page errors is the same story in a different costume.

## What not to do

- **Do not freeze the drive.** That is a myth that cracks platters.
- **Do not keep hitting Reset** until it “comes back”.
- **Do not install a miracle recovery app on the sick disk.** Install nothing on it. You overwrite the files you want.
- **Do not send it to a random postage address** from an advert if the data is irreplaceable without asking how they image.

If you already have a [backup](/blog/backup-pc-guide-wickford-essex), this is a hardware swap, not a crisis. If you do not, treat it as a crisis. Ransomware plus a clicking disk is two disasters; power off and use the [ransomware steps](/blog/ransomware-help-wickford-essex) only after the machine is isolated.

## Towers vs laptops

Desktop clicks are usually the obvious 3.5" disk. Laptops hide a 2.5" drive under a hatch or the whole bottom panel. Either way, I would rather open it than have you drill screws in the kitchen.

Custom PCs that click after a move often have a loose SATA cable, not a dead disk. Still stop using it until someone looks. A cable rattle plus a dying disk is how people lose the family archive the week after they “fixed the wire”. Gaming towers that also stutter belong with the [FPS guide](/blog/fix-pc-game-stuttering-fps-drops-essex) *after* the disk is safe.
