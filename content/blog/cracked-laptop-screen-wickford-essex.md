A backpack zip, a kitchen worktop, a schoolbag on the C2C. One knock is all it takes. Suddenly the lid has a spider-web of cracks, a black “ink blot” is spreading across the picture, or the display is a set of coloured lines.

A cracked laptop screen looks catastrophic. In most cases it is not. Your files usually sit on a drive that has nothing to do with the panel, and a proper replacement is almost always cheaper than a new machine. In Wickford we replace cracked, bleeding and flickering screens for homes and businesses across South Essex every week. Here is what to do next.

> **DO THIS FIRST**
>
> - **DO NOT** press, poke, or “flex” the glass to see if it still works. That turns a small LCD leak into a full black-out.
> - **DO NOT** peel shattered glass off a MacBook or touchscreen. Loose shards cut ribbon cables and fingers.
> - **DO** shut the lid gently if the glass is loose, and stop using the laptop on your lap until the panel is secure.
> - **DO** plug in an external monitor if you still need files, emails, or school work today. The computer itself is usually fine.

## What kind of screen damage are you looking at?

Not every “dead” display is the same repair. The type of damage tells you whether to stop using it today.

### Spider-web cracks in the glass

Common on school Chromebooks, HP and Dell lids, and anything that lives in a rucksack. The picture may still be visible underneath. The glass is the weak outer layer. Once it is cracked, more pressure can punch through into the LCD underneath.

### Black blobs, “oil slicks”, or bleeding pixels

That is liquid crystal leaking inside the panel. It almost never stays small. Keep using it and the blot spreads until the whole screen is unreadable. Power down if you can work from another device, and book a panel swap rather than waiting it out.

### Vertical or horizontal lines, flicker, or a dim/pink picture

This is often a failing LCD, a loose display cable in the hinge, or a backlight issue. It is still a screen or cable job. It is worth diagnosing properly so you are not quoted for a full panel when a hinge cable is the real fault.

## Will I lose my photos, coursework, or work files?

Almost never. A screen replacement swaps the panel. Your SSD or hard drive, your Windows or macOS profile, and your documents stay where they are. If the laptop still powers on, your data is already on the inside.

If the lid will not show a picture at all, try a spare HDMI or USB-C monitor. Seeing your desktop on the TV is a good sign the motherboard and storage are healthy. If nothing appears on any screen, that is a different fault, and we will tell you that before you pay for a panel.

## Why do DIY kits and cheap panels go wrong?

Modern lids are not the clip-in screens from 2012. Panels are glued, hinge covers hide tiny ribbon cables, and MacBook assemblies include the camera and Wi-Fi antennas. A mismatched 30-pin versus 40-pin connector, or a “compatible” panel with the wrong backlight, leaves you with a dim, pink, or dead display and a laptop that is harder to save.

Almost never. The panel comes off and a new one goes on. The drive and your login stay as they are.

### Should I keep using a cracked screen?

No, if the glass is loose or a black blot is spreading. Shut the lid gently and use an external monitor if you still need the files today. Do not press the glass to test it.
