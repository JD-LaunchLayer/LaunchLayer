A **laptop charger not working** is three different objects that get blamed as one. The brick, the cable, and the socket on the laptop fail in different ways, and they are not the same bill.

At the Wickford bench I separate them before anyone buys a “universal” charger from a marketplace. The wrong voltage on a barrel tip, or a 30W phone brick on a laptop that wants 65W, is how a cheap lead becomes a board fault. If the machine runs on the mains but the battery percentage never moves, that checklist is the [battery not charging guide](/blog/laptop-battery-not-charging-wickford/) — this page stops at the lead and the port.

## Other socket, then a borrowed charger

Do these two checks before you decide the laptop is dead.

1. **Another wall socket.** Not the same four-way block under the desk. Cheap strips sag when a heater or a second laptop is on them.
2. **Borrow a known-good charger** of the same tip and the same wattage, or bring yours to the workshop and I will put a bench supply on it. If a second brick charges the laptop straight away, your supply is the fault. If neither brick does, we are looking at the port or further in.

Look at the LED on the brick, if it has one. No light, plus a cable that bends flat where it leaves the block, is usually the charger. That strain-relief crack is a fire risk as well as a charge fault. Do not tape it and keep it in a schoolbag.

USB-C leads fail on their own, even when the brick is fine. A frayed cable that “still clicks in” will drop power as soon as the laptop moves. Swap the lead before you condemn the port.

## The wiggle test — once

Plug in, then move the connector a few millimetres. If the charge light or the Windows icon flickers, the socket is loose. That is the useful result. Stop there. Rocking it for a week “because it still works if I prop the lead on a book” is how the jack tears the pads off the little board inside.

**Angle-dependent charging is a port handoff.** It charges flat on the table and dies when the lead hangs off a sofa. It charges with the plug pushed up, and not when it sits straight. I treat that as a DC jack or USB-C board, not as a battery, and not as “you need a new laptop”.

## Cable versus brick versus the laptop

| What you see | What it usually is |
| --- | --- |
| Brick LED dead, laptop fine on a borrowed charger | The charger |
| LED on, lead frayed, icon drops when the cable moves in the middle | The cable, not the socket |
| Icon only holds at one angle, or the plug feels sloppy in the chassis | DC jack or USB-C board |
| Any good charger runs the laptop, but the percentage never rises | Battery or charge reporting — see the battery checklist |

On a lot of 14" business laptops the charge socket is a small daughter-board. A snapped USB-C port is a board swap, not a write-off. Barrel jacks on older HP, Dell and Lenovo machines are the same idea: the plastic cracks, the socket pulls, the pads lift.
