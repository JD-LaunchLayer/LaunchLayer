If every document has a strange extension, a notepad file on the desktop demands Bitcoin, or the wallpaper has been replaced with a countdown, you are dealing with ransomware. It is not a “virus scan will fix it” pop-up. The files are encrypted.

This is different from a [phishing link you just clicked](/blog/clicked-phishing-link-emergency-guide) (act in minutes) and different from a [refund scam call](/blog/refund-scam-protection-billericay-brentwood). The damage is already on the disk. What you do next decides whether we restore from backup or talk about [data recovery](/data-recovery).

## Do this immediately

1. **Turn the machine off.** Not sleep. Hold the power button. Every extra minute can encrypt more shares and USB drives.
2. **Unplug USB backups.** If a “backup” stick is still mounted, it may already be toast — that is why the [backup guide](/blog/backup-pc-guide-wickford-essex) says to unplug them.
3. **Do not pay from the dining table.** Paying does not guarantee a key, marks you as a payer, and is a decision to take slowly with clear facts — not while a countdown timer shouts at you.
4. **Do not run a miracle decryptor you found on Facebook.** Some are legitimate research tools. Many are a second infection.

If this started after a remote-helper “from Microsoft”, read the [scam awareness post](/blog/scam-awareness) as well. The ransomware may be the second half of the same job.

## What I can and cannot do in Wickford

**Can:** isolate the PC, image the drive, check whether a known decryptor exists for that family, strip the malware, and restore from a **clean** backup or cloud version history. OneDrive and Google Drive sometimes keep older versions from before the encryption. That is the quiet happy ending.

**Cannot:** promise to reverse every modern strain without a backup. Anyone who guarantees that in an advert is selling hope.

If the only copy lived on that laptop, we treat it like a recovery case. Do not keep trying to “open” encrypted files. You can fragment what is left.

A blue screen in the middle of the chaos is not “Windows fighting back”. It is often the disk or a half-written encryptor. See the [blue screen guide](/blog/windows-blue-screen-fix-essex), then still power off.

## How it usually gets in (Essex homes and small firms)

- A fake delivery PDF.
- A compromised WeTransfer-style link.
- Remote Access software left behind after a scare call.
- An old Windows 10 box that never got a supported upgrade path and no longer gets security patches. The [Windows 10 end-of-support piece](/blog/windows-10-end-of-support-help-essex) is the background; an unpatched PC on the same Wi-Fi as your banking is the risk.

Passkeys and a password manager will not magically decrypt files, but they stop the next login sweep. The [passkeys note](/blog/switch-to-passkeys-2026-guide) is the follow-up once the machine is clean.

Small firms that kept invoices in one Documents folder should read [IT for solopreneurs](/blog/it-support-for-solopreneurs-wickford-essex) after we are out of the fire. Cleanup without a restore test is how you meet me again in six months.
