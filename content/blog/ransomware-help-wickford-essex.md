If every document has a strange extension, a notepad file on the desktop demands Bitcoin, or the wallpaper has been replaced with a countdown, you're dealing with ransomware. It isn't a "virus scan will fix it" pop-up. The files are encrypted.

This isn't the fixed-price virus clean-up at £69. Ransomware is quoted as cleanup plus restore once we've seen it.

This is different from a [phishing link you've just clicked](/blog/clicked-phishing-link-emergency-guide/) (act in minutes) and different from a [refund scam call](/blog/refund-scam-protection-billericay-brentwood/). The damage is already on the disk. What you do next decides whether we restore from backup or talk about [data recovery](/data-recovery/).

## Do this immediately

1. **Turn the machine off.** Not sleep. Hold the power button. Every extra minute can encrypt more shared folders and USB drives.
2. **Unplug USB backups.** If a "backup" stick is still plugged in, it may already be encrypted. That's why the [backup guide](/blog/backup-pc-guide-wickford-essex/) says to unplug them.
3. **Don't pay from the dining table.** Paying doesn't guarantee a key, it marks you as someone who pays, and it's a decision to take slowly with clear facts, not while a countdown shouts at you.
4. **Don't run a miracle decryptor you found on Facebook.** Some are legitimate research tools. Many are a second infection.

If this started after a remote helper "from Microsoft", read the [scam awareness post](/blog/scam-awareness/) as well. The ransomware may be the second half of the same job.

## What we can and can't do in Wickford

**Can:** isolate the PC, copy the drive, check whether a known decryptor exists for that family, remove the malware, and restore from a **clean** backup or cloud version history. OneDrive and Google Drive sometimes keep older versions from before the encryption. That's the quiet good outcome.

**Can't:** promise to reverse every modern strain without a backup. Anyone who guarantees that in an advert is selling hope.

If the only copy lived on that laptop, we treat it like a recovery case. Don't keep trying to "open" encrypted files. You can damage what's left.

A blue screen in the middle of the chaos isn't "Windows fighting back". It's often the disk, or an encryptor that stopped halfway. See the [blue screen guide](/blog/windows-blue-screen-fix-essex/), then still power off.

## How it usually gets in

- A fake delivery PDF.
- A compromised file-share link.
- Remote-access software left behind after a scare call.
- An old Windows 10 PC that never got a supported upgrade and isn't enrolled for security updates. The [Windows 10 end-of-support piece](/blog/windows-10-end-of-support-help-essex/) is the background. An unpatched PC on the same Wi-Fi as your banking is the risk.

Passkeys and a password manager won't decrypt files, but they stop the next login sweep. The [passkeys note](/blog/switch-to-passkeys-2026-guide/) is the follow-up once the machine is clean.

Small firms that kept invoices in one Documents folder should read [IT for solopreneurs](/blog/it-support-for-solopreneurs-wickford-essex/) after we're out of the fire. Cleanup without a restore test is how the same problem comes back.
