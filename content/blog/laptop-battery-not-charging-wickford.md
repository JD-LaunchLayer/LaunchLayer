## Quick checks

Try one step at a time.

1. **Another wall socket.** Use a different socket, not the same extension lead. A cheap strip can struggle when a heater or a second laptop is plugged into it.
2. **Reset the power.** Shut the laptop down, unplug the charger, and hold the power button for a few seconds. Plug the charger back in and switch on. This can clear a stuck "not charging" reading. It doesn't replace a failed part.
3. **Restart the laptop.** On Windows 11, "plugged in, not charging" sometimes clears after a normal restart.

## Charger and cable

Try a known-good charger with the same tip and the same wattage. If a second charger fills the laptop straight away, the charger's the fault. If neither does, we look at the port, or at a fault inside the laptop.

Look at the light on the brick, if it has one. No light, and a cable that bends flat where it leaves the block, usually means the charger has failed. A cracked strain relief is a fire risk as well as a charge fault. Don't tape it and keep using it.

A USB-C lead can fail on its own, even when the brick's fine. A frayed cable that still clicks in will drop power as soon as the laptop moves. Swap the lead before you decide the port has failed.

Match the wattage printed on the original brick. The wrong voltage on a barrel tip, or a 30W phone charger on a laptop that needs 65W, is how a cheap lead becomes a board fault.

## Charging port

Plug in, then move the connector a few millimetres. If the charge light or the Windows icon flickers, the socket's loose. That's the useful result. Stop there. Rocking the plug because it still works when you rest the lead on a book is how the jack tears the pads off the board inside.

If it only charges at one angle, the port's the fault. It charges flat on the table and stops when the lead hangs off a sofa. It charges with the plug pushed up, and not when it sits straight. That's a DC jack or USB-C board, not a battery, and not a reason to replace the laptop.

On many 14-inch business laptops the charge socket is a small board of its own. A snapped USB-C port is a board swap. It doesn't mean the laptop's finished. Barrel jacks on older HP, Dell and Lenovo machines work the same way. The plastic cracks, the socket pulls, and the pads lift.

## Battery and Windows charge settings

These checks are for a laptop that runs on the charger and shows the plug icon. The power's getting in. The question is whether the battery will fill and hold it.

Windows 11 can show the lead as connected and still say "not charging". The percentage can also sit at the same number all day, often around 80%.

On many Dell, Lenovo and HP laptops that stop is on purpose. A charge limit keeps the battery from filling all the way so it lasts longer. Turn the limit off in the manufacturer's app if you want a full charge. The setting's often called conservation mode or battery-health mode. It's a limit you can switch off, not a broken battery.

Windows can write a battery report. Open Command Prompt and type `powercfg /batteryreport`, then open the file it saves. It compares the battery's original size with what it holds now, which is clearer than the percentage in the taskbar.

A worn battery shows up as a percentage that falls from a lot to nothing in a few minutes, a laptop that switches off without warning on battery, or one that dies the moment you unplug a charger you trust. The report will usually show it holding far less than its original size.

If the laptop runs on the charger, the report looks healthy, and it still won't fill, the part passing power to the battery may be at fault. A new battery doesn't help there, which is why the free diagnostic comes before any part is ordered.

## FAQ

### Why is my laptop plugged in but not charging?

It can be a weak or loose charger, a dead wall socket, a loose port, a Windows charge limit, or a worn battery. Work through the checks above before you buy a part.

### Is it the charger, the port, or the battery?

Try a known-good charger with the same tip and wattage, in a different wall socket. If that charges the laptop, it was the charger or the socket. If the icon only appears when you hold the plug at an angle, or flickers when the lead moves, it's the port. If a charger you trust is in firmly and the laptop dies the moment you unplug it, it's the battery.
