A blue screen looks like the machine has died. Most of the time it has not. Windows hit a fault it could not recover from, wrote a crash dump, and restarted. The useful bit is **why** — and whether your files are still sitting safely on the disk.

I diagnose blue screens from Wickford homes and small offices every month. The pattern is almost never “Windows is broken, buy a new PC”. It is a driver, a dying disk, bad RAM, heat, or a half-finished update.

## Do this before you start clicking Reset

1. **Let it reboot once.** If it comes back, you still have a diagnosable machine.
2. **Photograph the QR / stop code** if it sits long enough. `KERNEL_DATA_INPAGE_ERROR`, `IRQL_NOT_LESS_OR_EQUAL` and `CRITICAL_PROCESS_DIED` point at different hardware.
3. **Do not run random “repair” EXEs** from the first Google ad. That is how a crash becomes a [malware cleanup](/wickford-virus-removal).
4. If it loops and never reaches the desktop, stop power-cycling it fifty times. That is how a weak disk gets worse.

If the PC is also clicking or grinding, skip this page and go straight to [data recovery](/data-recovery). Keep using it and you can turn a recoverable disk into a platter nobody can read. Pair that with the [data recovery blog](/blog/data-recovery-services-wickford-essex) if you want the longer explanation before you drive over.

## What the common stop codes actually mean on the bench

I am paraphrasing, not quoting a Microsoft manual:

- **DISK / INPAGE errors** — Windows could not read a page from storage. Classic failing HDD, a SATA cable in a dusty tower, or an SSD that has run out of spare blocks.
- **MEMORY_MANAGEMENT / PAGE_FAULT** — RAM. A cheap stick, a machine that sat in a hot conservatory, or a laptop that was dropped.
- **DRIVER / VIDEO TDR** — graphics driver or a GPU that is overheating. Gamers: this sits next to the [stuttering guide](/blog/fix-pc-game-stuttering-fps-drops-essex).
- **CRITICAL_PROCESS_DIED after an update** — a botched Windows 11 feature update, often on a machine that barely passed the checker. That is a backup-first job, not a second attempt at Setup.

Heat still matters. A tower stuffed with pet hair will blue-screen under Teams as happily as it will in a game. Pair this with the [dust and thermal notes for Basildon](/blog/pc-dust-thermal-servicing-basildon) if the crashes only happen after an hour.

A laptop that blue-screens only on battery, or only when the charger is wiggled, is sometimes power delivery rather than Windows. Use the [not charging guide](/blog/laptop-not-charging-wickford-essex) as well.

## Safe mode is a test, not a lifestyle

If you can boot Safe Mode and it is stable, we are looking at software or a GPU driver. If it still crashes in Safe Mode, we are looking at RAM, storage or the board. That split saves you buying a new Windows licence you do not need.

I keep a known-good RAM tester and disk imager on the Wickford bench so we are not gambling with your family photos. If the disk is the suspect, I image first. Always. A slow machine that has not crashed yet might only need an [SSD upgrade](/blog/laptop-ssd-upgrade-wickford-essex). A crashing one might need the image *before* anyone clones.

## When a blue screen is really a scam leftover

A subset of “my PC keeps crashing” tickets are leftover remote-access tools from a fake refund call. If you had a pop-up that told you to ring a number, read the [phishing first-hour guide](/blog/clicked-phishing-link-emergency-guide) and the [scam overview](/blog/scam-awareness) before we talk hardware. Uninstalling “support” software you never asked for is step one; Event Viewer is step two.

## What I will not do

I will not “reset Windows” as a first move on a disk that might be dying. Reset overwrites the evidence and sometimes the photos. I will not install a driver pack from a random site. I will not tell you a 2014 office tower with one stick of RAM is “fine, ignore the blue screens”.
