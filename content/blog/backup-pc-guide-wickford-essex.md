Data recovery exists because backups didn't. We'd rather you never need the [Wickford data recovery page](/data-recovery/). A backup is boring, cheap, and the reason a dead laptop is an inconvenience instead of a family argument.

This isn't [digital inheritance](/blog/secure-digital-inheritance-guide/) (who gets the photos when you're gone). This is a Tuesday: the disk dies, the laptop is stolen on the train, or a nasty PDF locks the Documents folder.

## The only rule that matters: 3-2-1, in English

- **3 copies** of anything you'd be upset to lose (the live files, plus two backups).
- **2 different types of media.** For example the PC plus a portable SSD, not two folders on the same disk.
- **1 copy off-site.** A cloud account you control, or a drive that lives at work or a relative's house, not in the same kitchen as the laptop.

One USB stick left plugged in all day isn't a backup. Ransomware encrypts that too. A "backup" folder on the same PC isn't a backup either.

## What to copy first (in this order)

1. **Photos and WhatsApp or iPhone exports.** People think of Word documents. They mean years of children.
2. **Tax, house, and school PDFs.**
3. **Outlook mail archives,** if you still keep mail on the PC. If you're still on a free Gmail address for a business, read the [professional email post](/blog/professional-business-email-setup-essex/) as well.
4. **Browser bookmarks and a password-manager export.** Better still, a manager that already syncs.
5. Programmes can be reinstalled. Licences and installers are useful. They aren't the emergency.

If the machine is already slow or clicking, **don't** run a full backup overnight and hope. Copy the disk on the bench. See [data recovery](/data-recovery/) and stop using the PC.

## Cloud is fine. A folder called Backup on the Desktop isn't.

OneDrive, Google Drive and iCloud are excellent **if they're actually signed in and actually syncing**. We see Wickford laptops where OneDrive shows a cloud icon and the important folder was set to "online only" on a machine that never finished the upload.

Open the cloud website from your phone. If last week's photos aren't there, they aren't backed up.

A cheap 1TB portable SSD that you plug in once a week is still the most reliable local copy we suggest for people who don't want a subscription. Label it. Put a reminder in the calendar. Unplug it when you're done.

Windows File History and a Mac Time Machine drive are fine for older copies of Documents. They aren't a substitute for checking the cloud website. We'd rather you had a slightly messy 3-2-1 than a tidy app you never opened.

If invoices live in one person's Documents folder, you don't have a business backup. You have a single point of failure. ## What a workshop backup looks like vs DIY

DIY is a portable SSD and a cloud account you control. A workshop job is a copy of the whole disk before we move it onto an [SSD](/blog/laptop-ssd-upgrade-wickford-essex/) or chase a [blue screen](/blog/windows-blue-screen-fix-essex/). Different tools, same idea: a copy that doesn't live only inside the sick machine.

If the laptop also won't hold a charge, bring the brick. That's the [charging guide](/blog/laptop-battery-not-charging-wickford/), not a backup problem. If it overheats every time File History runs, fix the dust first ([overheating](/blog/fix-overheating-laptop-wickford-essex/)).
