A backup is a second copy of your files, and setting one up is easier than most people expect. We'd rather you never needed the [Wickford data recovery page](/data-recovery/). A backup is boring and cheap, and it's the difference between a dead laptop being an inconvenience and it being a family argument.

This isn't about [digital inheritance](/blog/secure-digital-inheritance-guide/) (who gets the photos when you're gone). It's about an ordinary Tuesday: the disk dies, the laptop's stolen on the train, or a nasty PDF locks the Documents folder.

## The only rule that matters: 3-2-1, in English

- **3 copies** of anything you'd be upset to lose: the live files, plus two backups.
- **2 different types of media.** For example, the PC plus a portable SSD (a small, fast external drive), not two folders on the same disk.
- **1 copy off-site.** A cloud account you control, or a drive that lives at work or at a relative's house, not in the same kitchen as the laptop.

A USB stick left plugged in all day isn't a backup, because ransomware (a virus that locks your files) can scramble that too. A "backup" folder on the same PC isn't one either. If you ever need [Wickford virus removal](/wickford-virus-removal/) or help with a [blue screen](/blog/windows-blue-screen-fix-essex/), a recent backup makes it much less of a worry, because your files are already safe somewhere else.

## What to copy first (in this order)

1. **Photos and WhatsApp or iPhone exports.** People think of Word documents, but they usually mean years of family photos.
2. **Tax, house and school PDFs.**
3. **Outlook mail archives,** if you still keep mail on the PC. If your business still runs on a free Gmail address, read the [professional email post](/blog/professional-business-email-setup-essex/) as well.
4. **Browser bookmarks, and your passwords.** Use a password manager that syncs, so there's nothing to copy. If you do export your passwords, use an encrypted export, because a plain .csv export can be read by anyone who finds the file; delete any plain copy straight after.
5. Programs can be reinstalled. Licence keys and installers are useful to keep, but they aren't the emergency.

If the drive is clicking or grinding, stop using the PC and call us, or book a free diagnostic. If the PC is only slow or throwing errors, copy your photos and documents to a portable drive or your cloud account now, then book a free diagnostic. See [data recovery](/data-recovery/). ## Cloud backups are fine, once you've checked they're syncing

OneDrive, Google Drive and iCloud are excellent, **as long as they're signed in and actually syncing**.

Open the cloud website on your phone. If last week's photos aren't there, they aren't backed up.

For people who don't want a subscription, a portable SSD (what we suggest most) or an external hard drive that you plug in once a week is a simple local copy. Label it, put a reminder in the calendar, and unplug it when you're done.

Windows File History and a Mac Time Machine drive are fine for older versions of your documents, but they don't replace checking the cloud website.
