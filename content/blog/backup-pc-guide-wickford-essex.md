Data recovery exists because backups didn't happen. We'd rather you never needed the [Wickford data recovery page](/data-recovery/). A backup is boring and cheap, and it's the difference between a dead laptop being an inconvenience and it being a family argument.

This isn't about [digital inheritance](/blog/secure-digital-inheritance-guide/) (who gets the photos when you're gone). It's about an ordinary Tuesday: the disk dies, the laptop's stolen on the train, or a nasty PDF locks the Documents folder.

## The only rule that matters: 3-2-1, in English

- **3 copies** of anything you'd be upset to lose: the live files, plus two backups.
- **2 different types of media.** For example, the PC plus a portable SSD, not two folders on the same disk.
- **1 copy off-site.** A cloud account you control, or a drive that lives at work or at a relative's house, not in the same kitchen as the laptop.

A USB stick left plugged in all day isn't a backup, because ransomware can encrypt that too. A "backup" folder on the same PC isn't one either.

## What to copy first (in this order)

1. **Photos and WhatsApp or iPhone exports.** People think of Word documents, but they usually mean years of family photos.
2. **Tax, house and school PDFs.**
3. **Outlook mail archives,** if you still keep mail on the PC. If your business still runs on a free Gmail address, read the [professional email post](/blog/professional-business-email-setup-essex/) as well.
4. **Browser bookmarks and a password-manager export.** Better still, use a manager that already syncs.
5. Programs can be reinstalled. Licence keys and installers are useful to keep, but they aren't the emergency.

If the machine is already slow or clicking, **do not** run a full backup overnight and hope. Stop using the PC and see [data recovery](/data-recovery/). We'll assess the drive before any copy is attempted and explain the costs before work starts.

## Cloud is fine. A folder called Backup on the Desktop isn't.

OneDrive, Google Drive and iCloud are excellent, **as long as they're signed in and actually syncing**.

Open the cloud website on your phone. If last week's photos aren't there, they aren't backed up.

For people who don't want a subscription, a 1TB portable SSD that you plug in once a week is still the local copy we suggest most. Label it, put a reminder in the calendar, and unplug it when you're done.

Windows File History and a Mac Time Machine drive are fine for older versions of your documents, but they don't replace checking the cloud website. A slightly messy 3-2-1 is better than a tidy app you never opened.
