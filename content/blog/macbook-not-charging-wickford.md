A MacBook that is plugged in and not charging is often macOS holding the battery on purpose. The battery menu may say "Not Charging" or "Charging On Hold", the percentage may sit near 80%, or the MagSafe light may stay amber. Read what the Mac is telling you before you buy a cable or a battery.

## Checks to try first

Try one step at a time. Stop when the battery starts to fill.

1. **Read the battery menu.** "Charging On Hold", or a percentage sitting around 80%, is often Optimised Battery Charging or a Charge Limit. Choose "Charge to Full Now" if you need it full today.
2. **Match the wattage.** If the menu says "Not Charging" and the Mac still runs, the adapter may be too small. Use one of at least the wattage that came with the Mac.
3. **Swap the cable and the port.** Some USB-C cables carry data only and will not charge. Try another charging cable, then another port.
4. **Look at the MagSafe light,** on a Mac that has one. Amber means charging or on hold. Green means full.

## Not Charging or Charging On Hold

Open the battery menu. "Charging On Hold" means Optimised Battery Charging is waiting. A Charge Limit can hold charging around 80% as well. When you need the battery full, choose "Charge to Full Now" from that menu.

Battery health management can pause charging until the battery drops to 93% or lower. That pause is the Mac looking after the pack. It is not a failed battery.

## Not Charging on a low-wattage adapter

"Not Charging" can also mean the adapter is too small. The Mac runs on the power it is getting, and the battery does not fill. Use an adapter of at least the wattage of the one that came with the Mac. A phone charger that happens to fit is a common cause of this.

## What the MagSafe 3 light means

On a Mac with MagSafe 3, the light on the connector is amber while charging is underway or charging is on hold. It turns green when the battery is fully charged. Amber with the percentage sitting still is often the hold above, not a broken port.

MagSafe 3 is on MacBook Pro models introduced in 2021 or later, except the 13-inch M2 MacBook Pro from 2022, and on MacBook Air models introduced in 2022 or later. Earlier MacBooks use an older MagSafe, or USB-C only. A MagSafe 3 lead does not fit those older chargers.

The Mac charges from one port at a time. A second plug, in MagSafe or in USB-C, does not make it charge faster. It uses the source that provides the most power.

## USB-C cables that will not charge

Some USB-C cables carry data only and will not charge the Mac. If the Mac does not see the adapter, try another charging cable and another port. Use a cable that is meant to charge, with an adapter of at least the original wattage.

## Service Recommended

Open Battery settings and look at the battery condition. "Service Recommended" means the battery needs replacing. Check the cable and the adapter first, so a worn pack is not blamed for a lead that never charged.
