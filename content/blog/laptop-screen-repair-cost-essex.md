## Labour and the panel are separate lines

The £99 is the work: removing the bezel, working carefully around the hinges, fitting the matching panel, and testing the lid through a full open and close. It's a **flat £99** for labour on Windows laptops and Chromebooks.

The second line is the panel itself. We price it for your model, and you see that price before it's ordered. A basic HD panel and a touchscreen assembly don't cost the same, so we don't publish a table that pretends every lid is identical. If we don't have your exact part number yet, we'll quote it once we do.

MacBook lids are a different assembly. Fitting for those is **from £85 plus the panel**, so an Apple quote is built differently from the Windows £99 line. That starting point is on the [Wickford screen page](/laptop-screen-repair-wickford/).

Most jobs are ready in **1 to 3 working days**, depending on how quickly the UK supplier can ship that panel.

## Why two cracked laptops aren't the same total

The labour figure barely moves. The panel price does, for ordinary reasons:

- **The model.** A school Chromebook and a 15" touchscreen don't use the same part.
- **Touch or not.** A touch layer is a different assembly from a plain LCD.
- **What else broke.** Lines, flicker or a dim picture can come from the panel or from a display cable problem, and we'd rather find a cable fault than sell you a panel you don't need. A twisted lid can also mean hinge damage.
- **The machine around the screen.** A cracked lid on a laptop that also won't charge is two jobs, because screen labour doesn't include a battery or a charging port.

That's why a neighbour's invoice isn't your quote. Diagnostics are free, so the split is clear before you agree to anything.

Your files almost always stay where they are, because a panel swap doesn't wipe the drive. If the lid shows nothing at all, connecting an external monitor shows whether the rest of the laptop is working before anyone pays for a panel.
