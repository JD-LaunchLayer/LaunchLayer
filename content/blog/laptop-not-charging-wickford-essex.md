The cable is in. The socket has power. Windows still says “not charging”, or the machine dies the second you pull the lead. That is one of the most common drop-offs at the Wickford bench, and it is three different faults that get sold as “you need a new laptop”.

At **LaunchLayer** I separate **the brick**, **the port**, and **the battery** before anyone spends money. Buying a random charger off a marketplace is how a £20 problem becomes a £200 board.

## Quick checks you can do in two minutes

- **Try another wall socket**, not an overloaded extension. Cheap strips drop voltage under load.
- **Look at the LED on the brick** (if it has one). Dead LED plus a floppy cable at the strain relief usually means the charger, not the laptop.
- **Wiggle the barrel or USB-C plug.** If the charge icon flickers, the DC jack or USB-C board is loose — very common on school and van laptops.
- **Feel the underside by the battery.** Hot, tight, or a bulging palm rest is a [swollen battery](/blog/laptop-battery-replacement-wickford). Unplug it and do not keep charging.

If the machine will not turn on at all, even on the mains, use the [won’t turn on guide](/blog/laptop-not-turning-on-slow-fix) instead of this one.

## Fault 1: the charger (the easy win)

USB-C PD chargers are not all equal. A phone brick that “fits” may only offer 20–30W. A 65W laptop will trickle, get hot, or refuse to charge while you work. Match the wattage printed on the original supply.

Barrel chargers (older HP, Dell, Lenovo) need the right tip **and** the right voltage. A 19V machine with a 20V-ish mystery brick can brown-out the board. I keep known-good testers on the bench so we are not guessing from a photo.

A cracked strain-relief — the rubber where the cable meets the brick — is a fire risk as well as a charge fault. Do not wrap the cable tight around the block and leave it in a schoolbag. If the brick is the only issue, you walk out the same day.

## Fault 2: the port or the cable inside the hinge

On a lot of 14" business laptops the charge socket is a tiny daughter-board, not the motherboard. That is good news: a snapped USB-C port is a board swap, not a write-off.

If the lead has been yanked in a rucksack every schoolday, I also check the internal DC cable. The plastic around the jack cracks, then the socket rips the pads off. Same abuse pattern as a lid that no longer sits straight — if the chassis is twisting when you plug in, stop forcing it.

Liquid plus charging is a different emergency. If tea went near the port, follow the [spill steps](/blog/spilled-water-on-laptop-fix-essex) and do not keep plugging it in. Corrosion on a USB-C port looks like “it charges on Tuesdays”.

## Fault 3: the battery reporting “plugged in, not charging”

Windows will often refuse to charge a pack that has failed its health checks. The machine then runs only on the adapter and dies if you knock the plug. That is still a battery job, not a new laptop, unless the pack has already swollen.

I will not force-charge a puffed cell. That is how you get a warped chassis and a fire risk. If the trackpad is lifting or the palm rest is proud of the keyboard, that is the battery post linked above, not a software reset.

Some HP and Lenovo models also sit at 0% or 100% forever because a cheap charger confused the fuel gauge. A known-good brick plus a battery reset on the bench usually sorts it. A YouTube “hold the power button for 60 seconds” ritual is harmless; it is not a repair.

## What this is not

A slow laptop that still charges is probably storage or heat, not the port. If it boots like 2014, read the [SSD upgrade guide](/blog/laptop-ssd-upgrade-wickford-essex). If it only dies after an hour of Teams with the fans screaming, that is [overheating](/blog/fix-overheating-laptop-wickford-essex).
