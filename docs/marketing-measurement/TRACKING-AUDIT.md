## Duplicate scripts and inflated counting
- **Umami tag:** exactly 1 per page on all 101 HTML files, and the live HTML on 4 sampled pages also shows 1. No duplicate tags and no second analytics tool.
- **Event double-fire:** none found. Each tracked `<a>` has one `data-umami-event`. No JS `umami.track` call duplicates the click events; the only JS call is the form event, which is not attached to a click. No tracked elements are nested.
- **Inflation risks found:**
  1. **Automated traffic.** About 88% of visitors are outside the UK, mostly SG and US data centres, with 88% bounce. The 8 Oct spike was 60 of 75 pageviews from SG.
  2. **Internal and diagnostic visits** (`diag=headless`, `diag=curl`, and install-day testing on 27 Sep). Umami has no IP exclusion on the share view.
  3. **404 pageviews** (18) count as pageviews because `404.html` carries the tag.
  4. **GSC page split** across www/non-slash and apex/slash URLs. Merge them before ranking pages.
- **Undercounting risks:** ad and tracker blockers block `cloud.umami.is` for some users. Taps on call links are not calls, and calls dialled straight from GBP or from memory never touch the site.

## What Umami cannot do
- It cannot confirm a real enquiry, a call connection, a booking or a job. It records browser events only.
- It cannot attribute GBP or Maps actions that never reach the website (calls and direction requests made in Maps).
- It cannot identify people. By design there are no names, emails or cross-device identity.
- It has no data before 27 Sep 2026.
- Out of the box it doesn't separate bots from humans, so we use a country=GB filter as a proxy. This proxy misses UK bots and UK VPN users.
